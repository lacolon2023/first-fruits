How First Fruits supports you

Everything a busy parent needs to teach with confidence

Most curricula hand you materials and wish you luck. We wrap every lesson in the support that makes it actually doable at your kitchen table.

Open-and-go lessons

Every lesson is scripted with a clear objective, the words to say, and the steps to follow. Prep is about five minutes — gather a few household items and go.

“Teach the teacher” phonics

The part parents fear most, made simple. Short, plain-English notes explain how to teach letter sounds, blending, and segmenting — the Science of Reading, without the jargon.

Scope & sequence

See the whole year at a glance — every unit, week, and skill, in the order they build. No guessing what comes next or whether you've covered enough.

Simple progress tracking

A weekly checklist marks what your child has mastered — and doubles as the records many states ask homeschoolers to keep. Celebrate growth without the spreadsheet.

Placement & “where do I start?”

Not sure which grade fits, or new mid-year? A quick placement guide points you to the right starting unit so your child isn't bored or lost.

Supply lists & shopping links

Each unit lists the few everyday items you'll need, with links — so there are no surprises and no last-minute runs to the store.

A lesson, start to finish

Your part is simple

Here's exactly what teaching a First Fruits lesson looks like for you — about 15–20 minutes of core teaching, plus hands-on play.

1

Open the lesson

Read the one big idea, the verse, and today's objective — it's all on the page.

2

Follow the script

Say the prompts, do the see-it / hear-it / do-it steps. We tell you what to do.

3

Play & practice

A quick hands-on activity or game makes it stick — and makes it fun.

4

Check the box

Mark the skill on the weekly checklist. That's your progress and your records, done.
