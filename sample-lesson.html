“Who I Am in Christ” — Kindergarten, Unit 1, Week 1

See exactly how a First Fruits lesson works: one big idea, three ways in. The same truth reaches every child — visual, auditory, and hands-on — integrating Scripture, phonics, and play into one cohesive lesson. About 15–20 minutes per core lesson, plus hands-on — minimal prep.

Big idea: I am made in the image of God.
Memory verse: “Be that new person who was made to be like God, truly good and pleasing to him.” — Ephesians 4:24 (ERV)
This week’s phonics: Mon m · Tue s · Wed a · Thu t · Fri Review  |  High-frequency words: I, am, the
See it · Visual
What your child sees
  • Mirror card: “God made ME”
  • Letter poster: m — “Mighty Maker made me”
  • Sight words on the wall: I, am, the
  • Picture sort: which words start with /m/? (moon, monkey, mountain)
  • Watch: the Creation, day by day
Why it works
  • Print-rich cues anchor the letter, the words, and the big idea before writing begins.
Hear it · Auditory
What your child hears & says
  • Memory-verse song: Ephesians 4:24
  • Say the sounds: /m/ /m/ — Mighty Maker
  • Read-aloud: God made me (Genesis 1)
  • Blend & segment aloud: am · mat · map
  • Repeat & clap: “I — am — special”
Why it works
  • Phonemic awareness — the Science of Reading core — is built through the ear first.
Do it · Hands-on
What your child does
  • Creation BINGO + sequencing cards
  • Trace & build the letter m
  • Decodable reader: highlight “m” words; heart the sight words
  • Mirror activity: “I see someone God made”
  • Feelings cards: “When I feel ___, I can go to God for ___.”
Why it works
  • Movement and play turn the lesson into memory — and fold in social-emotional growth.
🎧 Parent Coach Blending “map” — you don’t have to be a reading teacher
  • Say: “My turn: /m/ /ă/ /p/ … map. Now you.”
  • Listen for: smooth blending — no extra “uh” between the sounds.
  • If they guess from the picture: cover it and point back to the letters.
  • If they struggle: model once, do it together once, then let them try.
  • Ready to move on: your child reads 4 of 5 words without your help.
